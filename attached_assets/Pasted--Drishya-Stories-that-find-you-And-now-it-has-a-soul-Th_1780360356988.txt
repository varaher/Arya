Not inspired by Indian texts. **Rooted in them.**

```
The Vedas → The oldest stories ever told

The Mahabharata → The world's greatest screenplay
The Ramayana → Character arcs no Hollywood writer has matched
The Panchatantra → The original short story format
The Jataka Tales → 547 stories of lives and lessons
Kalidasa → Poetry that becomes visual
Tagore → The modern Indian soul
Premchand → The voice of ordinary India
```

Every story Drishya tells — **traces back to this river.**

**What This Means in Practice**

A child asks for a bedtime story.

Drishya doesn't generate a random tale.

It reaches into the **Panchatantra** — finds the wisdom thread — and tells it in the voice of a Kerala grandmother, in Malayalam, set in a coconut grove, with a crow and a clever child.

**Same story. 2000 years old. Feels like tonight.**

---

A filmmaker wants a script about betrayal.

Drishya doesn't start from zero.

It asks —

> *"Should we approach this through the Karna lens — the loyal man betrayed by his own devotion? Or the Vibhishana lens — the man who betrays for dharma?"*

**The filmmaker just got a script AND a philosophy.**

**The Philosophical Core**

Indian storytelling was never entertainment first.

It was always —
- **Dharma** — what is right
- **Artha** — what is meaningful
- **Kama** — what is felt
- **Moksha** — what sets free

Every Drishya story should carry **at least one of these threads.**

Invisibly. Naturally. The way the old stories always did.
